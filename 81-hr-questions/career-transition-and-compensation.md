# T8101 · HR Recruiter Round: Elevator Pitch, Leveling Calibration & Negotiation

**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Companies Asked:** All major product companies & startups  
**Interview Frequency:** ★★★★★  
**Category:** HR Interview  
**Concepts:** hr-interview, recruiter-screen, salary-negotiation, leveling, career-pitch  

## Question

How do you structure a compelling 2-minute elevator pitch for recruiter screens, how do you handle early compensation questions without anchoring yourself below market value, and what factors determine leveling calibration (Senior vs Staff vs Principal)?

## Expected Answer

1. **2-Minute Elevator Pitch**:
   - **Present (30s)**: Current role, seniority, tech stack, and primary business impact.
   - **Past (45s)**: Key career trajectory highlights, major scaling achievements, and architectural domain expertise.
   - **Future (45s)**: Why you are interested in this specific role and company, aligned with their technical scale or product mission.
2. **Compensation Handling**: Never share your past salary or give an early uncalibrated target number. Frame compensation preferences around market rates for the target level (`"I am looking for a competitive market package for a Senior/Staff Frontend Engineer level at your company"`).
3. **Leveling Calibration**:
   - **Senior (L5 / IC5)**: Executes complex projects independently, mentors peers, drives domain excellence.
   - **Staff (L6 / IC6)**: Multi-team technical scope, defines architectural standards, solves high ambiguity, aligns business strategy.

## Deep Explanation

### 1. Elevator Pitch Template Script

```
"I'm currently a Senior Frontend Engineer at Acme, where I lead our core Web Performance & UI Architecture team. Over the past 3 years, I've driven our migration to Next.js App Router and reduced page load times by 70%, which boosted conversion by 14%.

Before this, I spent 4 years building real-time data visualization dashboards using React and TypeScript at DataCorp. I've specialized heavily in performance optimization, micro-frontend architecture, and modern state management.

I'm excited about [Target Company] because your team is solving unique real-time collaborative editing challenges at immense user scale, which directly matches my background in complex browser rendering architectures."
```

### 2. Strategic Recruiter Responses

```
Recruiter: "What are your current compensation expectations?"

Response A (Decline Anchor):
"Right now, I'm focused on finding the right role fit and understanding the scope of your engineering challenges. Once we align on leveling through the technical rounds, I'm confident we can agree on a market-competitive package for that level."

Response B (Range Anchor):
"Based on market research for Senior/Staff Frontend roles in [Location/Remote], I am seeing total compensation ranges between $X and $Y. Is that within your target budget for this position?"
```

## Best Practices

- Always research leveling bands on levels.fyi before recruiter screens.
- Prepare clear, positive reasons for leaving your current company (growth, new scale, technical domain challenge) — never criticize past employers.
- Ask strategic questions at the end of recruiter calls (e.g. *"What are the biggest technical challenges the team is facing over the next 6 months?"*).

## Common Mistakes

- Disclosing past salary, which anchors negotiation leverage downward.
- Rambling chronologically through your entire resume instead of highlighting top impactful achievements.
